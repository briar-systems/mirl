# x86-64 selection rules

`mirl.target.register.x86.rules.TABLE` is the selection table for x86-64 (see [selection](../machine/select.md)). It is static data. It names rows of masc's x86 catalog and nothing else, so mirl never encodes and never invents an instruction.

## What it reads

- The extensions come from the target's selection, which masc closes over the levels and implied features once. A rule emits rows, and the engine drops a rule whose row masc does not admit. So SSE4.1 (`roundss`, `pmovsx`, `pmovzx`, `pmulld`), LZCNT, BMI1's `tzcnt`, POPCNT and FMA3 are each a row's requirement and never a branch in the table. Where two rules match, the cheaper wins, so the SSE4.1 row is taken where it is admitted and the SSE2 sequence otherwise.
- A rule names each row by the handle masc generates for it (`gen_row_index.ADD_GPR32_GPR32_01_O32`) and each class by its index handle (`gen_register.GPR64_CLASS`). The APX and EVEX forms of a row are never named.

## Values

- Integers, conditions and pointers are held in `gpr64`, floats in `xmm`, and vectors in `xmm` or, where the function's selection holds AVX, in `ymm`, whose low 128 bits each `xmm` is a view of. A value is one virtual register of `gpr64`, and a row names it through the view of the width it runs at, `eax`, `ax` or `al` of one location, so one value read as i8, i32 and i64 is never copied between views.
- An integer narrower than 64 bits leaves the bits above it undefined. A condition is 0 or 1 in the whole register. A byte or word operation whose result needs no extension runs at 32 bits, whose write zero extends, so it ends the whole value. A narrower write is a tie, read and written, so no result is ever partly written.
- x86's two-operand forms read and write their first operand, which a rule states as a tie of the result to the operand it replaces. An instruction that merges into its destination, as `cvtsi2sd`, `sqrtss` or `roundss`, follows a whole write of the destination, `movd` or `movaps`.
- The flags are no class. A compare and the branch, `setcc` or `cmov` that reads them are one expansion, and masc's rows state what each reads and writes of `rflags`. A condition held in a register is tested with `test`.
- A comparison clears its result before the compare, then `setcc` writes its low byte. A branch on a compare is `jcc` to the first edge and `jmp` to the second. A float equality is two conditions, zero and no parity, joined.
- Division and the high half of a product use masc's fixed operands: the dividend in `rax`, extended into `rdx` by `cdq`/`cqo` or a zero, the quotient read from `rax` and the remainder from `rdx`. A variable shift moves its count into `ecx` and shifts by `cl`. A byte or a word divides at 32 bits, which the narrow legalisation makes, since the byte and word forms divide `ah:al` and `dx:ax`.
- A binary64 is loaded and stored by `movq`, which writes what SSE `movsd` does and which Intel lists as data operand independent where it does not list `movsd`'s `0f 10` and `0f 11` forms (Intel 768547, Table 1), so a secret float moves in constant-time code. A declassify of a float zeroes its register above the value, `movq` for a binary64 and `pslldq` then `psrldq` by 12 for a binary32, so the point publishes no bit above it.
- A float constant is loaded from read-only data, which the pool legalisation makes before selection. A constant an expansion needs, as a sign mask or the bound of a saturating conversion, is built in a general register and moved across.

## Conversions

- Float to integer saturates without a branch: round toward zero, the destination's least or greatest value past its range and 0 for a nan. `cvttss2si` and `cvttsd2si` give the integer indefinite value past the range of their width and for a nan, which `cmov`s on compares against the bound fix up: the greatest value at or above 2^(w-1), and 0 where `ucomis` of the operand with itself is unordered. A narrow signed destination clamps the saturated word. An unsigned destination below 64 bits converts at 64 bits, clamps a negative, a nan or an out of range result to 0, then a value at 2^w or above to the greatest. A 64-bit unsigned destination converts the value less 2^63 where it is at 2^63 or above and sets the top bit.
- Integer to float extends a narrow operand to 32 bits, converts an unsigned 32-bit operand at 64 bits, and converts a 64-bit unsigned operand with its top bit set halved, its low bit kept for the rounding, then doubled.

## Addresses, calls and constants

- A load or a store folds its address into the memory operand: `[b]`, `[b + d]`, `[b + i]`, `[b + i*s]` and `[b + i*s + d]`, the scale a multiply or a shift by a power of two up to 8, and a symbol's address rip-relative with its displacement. `ptr.add` is `lea`.
- The address of a symbol follows the target's relocation model (see [ir/target.md](../ir/target.md)). A symbol reached by its distance from the code, or absolutely, which the small code model keeps within 2 GiB, is rip-relative (`pc32`): an address constant and `func.addr` are `lea`, and a load or a store folds it. A preemptible symbol is a `mov` from its global offset table slot, ELF's `gotpcrel32` and Mach-O's `got_pc32`, and a load or a store goes through the register. Selection states the relocation family and never a refined kind, which masc's encoder refuses: the encoder writes ELF's as its relaxable form for the instruction (`rex_gotpcrel32_relaxable` in a REX `mov`) and Mach-O's as `got_load_pc32` in a 64-bit `mov` load. Neither relocation carries an offset, so an address past its symbol's start loads the symbol's slot and adds the offset after, an `add` of a 32-bit immediate or of a register a 64-bit `mov` fills ([select.md](../machine/select.md)). A call is `call` with `branch32` under every model, which the format routes through the procedure linkage table.
- A thread-local variable follows the access the model declares. Local exec is a `mov` of the variable's offset from the thread pointer (`tpoff32`) and an `add` of `fs:[0]`, and initial exec a rip-relative `mov` of that offset from the global offset table (`gottpoff32`, which the encoder refines to its `code_4` or `code_6` form under rex2 or evex) and the same `add`. A pc-relative kind is stated only in a rip-relative address and an absolute one only outside one, so `tpoff32` is the `mov`'s immediate. Darwin's variable access is a rip-relative `mov` of the descriptor into rdi (`tlv_pc32`) and a `call` through its thunk (`Callee.descriptor`), which gives the address in rax and leaves only rax, rdi, the flags and the sse registers undefined. Local exec keeps an address's offset past its variable in `tpoff32`, and initial exec and the darwin access add it after the address is formed. Windows' TLS index access loads the module's index from `_tls_index`, the thread's table of module blocks from `gs:[0x58]`, the module's block from its entry, and adds the variable's `secrel32` offset. The loads of `fs:[0]`, `gs:[0x58]` and the table entry read what the system alone writes and are stated public to the constant-time walk (`Emit.system`), and `_tls_index` is declared a constant, as only the loader writes it. General dynamic is not selected.
- A direct call is `call rel32` under a branch relocation, and an indirect call `call` through a register. A call's clobbers are its convention row's.
- A copy or fill of a known length past a floor is `rep movsb` (destination in `rdi`, source in `rsi`, length in `rcx`) or `rep stosb` (destination in `rdi`, byte in `al`, length in `rcx`), at any length past it. The capability row states the floors (`bulk.copy` is `ascending{16}` and `bulk.fill` is `known{32}`): at or below them the bulk legalisation's line of 16-byte units is no larger than the instruction as selected and has no startup cost. `rep movsb` with its operands is 13 bytes against 6 for one unit copied and 14 for two, so its floor is 16 bytes, and `rep stosb` is 15 against 13 for two zeroed units and 20 for three, so its floor is 32. A copy is `rep movsb` only where it may ascend. One that may only descend or states neither order is the bulk legalisation's units, a line that reads every unit before it writes one up to its bound and a loop that picks its direction from the addresses past it. A copy or fill of a length not known is never a string instruction: the bulk legalisation moves it in packed SSE2 loops of 16 bytes a step (see [ir/target.md](../ir/target.md)), and so is every copy or fill the vectoriser makes of a loop, as no fold after it makes its length known (mirl#751). A volatile one calls the helper, and a kept fill is the bulk legalisation's kept stores, never `rep stosb`. No instruction mach emits sets the direction flag, so it is clear wherever a copy is interrupted, as the convention requires of every entry.

## Atomics

x86-64 is TSO. An atomic load is a plain load at every ordering, and an atomic store a plain store for relaxed and release and `xchg` for seq_cst. An add is `lock xadd`, a sub `lock xadd` of the negation, an exchange `xchg` and a compare and exchange `lock cmpxchg` with the expected value in the accumulator. A compare and exchange of two words, which the wide legalisation makes of a 128-bit one under cx16, is `lock cmpxchg16b`, its expected words moved into `rax` and `rdx`, its desired ones into `rbx` and `rcx`, and the words found taken out of `rax` and `rdx`, which the row reads and writes implicitly, with `setz` the success flag. And, or, exclusive or, nand, minimum and maximum are `cas` loops around `lock cmpxchg` (`mirl.target.register.x86.loop`). Every fence is `mfence`.

## Vectors

An SSE2 vector is held in one `xmm` register. The integer adds and subtracts of each lane width, the bitwise operations, `pmullw`, `pmulld` under SSE4.1 and two `pmuludq` below it, and the float arithmetic of each format are selected. A product of bytes is two `pmullw`, of the even bytes and of the odd ones shifted down, merged, and of quadwords three `pmuludq`, the low halves' product plus the cross products shifted up 32. A lane is widened by `pmovsx` and `pmovzx` under SSE4.1, and by `punpckl` with zeros or with the lane's sign below it. Loads and stores move 16, 8 or 4 bytes.

A comparison is selected with the sign extension of its mask as one pattern, the extension the root (see [ir/target.md](../ir/target.md)): `pcmpeq` and `pcmpgt` of each width, `pcmpeqq` under SSE4.1 and `pcmpeqd`, `pshufd` and `pand` below it, `pcmpgtq` under SSE4.2, an unsigned ordering as `pminu` and `pcmpeq` (`pminub` always, `pminuw` and `pminud` under SSE4.1), `psubusw` and a compare with zero for 16-bit lanes below it, and both sign bits flipped then `pcmpgt` for 32-bit lanes below SSE4.1 and 64-bit lanes under SSE4.2, an ordered comparison of 64-bit lanes below SSE4.2 as `pcmpgtd` and `pcmpeqd` with the low doublewords' sign flipped (both doublewords' for an unsigned one), the high halves' order unless they are equal and then the low halves', spread by `pshufd`, and `cmpps` and `cmppd` with `eq`, `lt`, `le` and `neq` for the float compares, whose answer on a nan is the scalar's. A negation of the mask is `pxor` with the all-ones `pcmpeqd`, as the complement is. A shift of 32- or 64-bit lanes is `vpsllvd`, `vpsllvq`, `vpsrlvd`, `vpsrlvq` and `vpsravd` under AVX2. Otherwise a shift of 16-, 32- or 64-bit lanes is `psll`, `psrl` and `psra` by a register: each lane's count is moved zero-extended into the low quadword of a register by `pslldq` and `psrldq` (`movq` for the first quadword), the whole vector is shifted by it, and the lane is taken from that shift, words paired into doublewords by `pslld`, `psrld` and `por`, and doublewords and quadwords merged by `punpcklqdq`, `punpckhqdq` and `pshufd`, so no lane passes through a general register. Where the count is one value in every lane, as `mirl.ir.uniform` reads it off how the count is made, a single shift by its first lane does. All of these saturate a count at or past the width as the ir's shifts do, the count read unsigned. Which of them is chosen never decides whether the shift is packed. SSE has no byte shift and no 64-bit arithmetic shift below AVX-512, so those are sequences (`mirl.target.register.x86.sse`): a byte shift takes one bit of the count at a time, bit k moved to each byte's sign by `psllw` by 7 - k and spread by `pcmpgtb` against zero to pick the lane shifted by 2^k (`paddb` for a left shift, `psrlw` and a byte mask for a right one), and a count past 7 clears the lane (`pminub` and `pcmpeqb` against 7), and an arithmetic shift of bytes or quadwords is the logical shift of the value flipped by its sign, flipped back, so a count past the width gives the sign. The SSE2 unit's operation rows (`X86_64_OPERATIONS`) state what the table selects, so the vector legalisation leaves those packed and takes every other lane by lane (#467).

Two 256-bit units follow SSE2's (`X86_64_UNITS`), both holding a vector of 32 bytes whole in one `ymm` register: AVX2's, of integer and float lanes, and AVX's, on a build without AVX2, of float lanes. A vector of 16 bytes or fewer still splits to SSE2's unit, stated first. Where the function's selection holds AVX, every vector is one virtual register of `ymm` (the hold `vector → ymm`), and a row of 16 bytes or fewer names it through its `xmm` view. A rule's position takes a vector only where a class it admits has room for all of the vector's bits ([select.md](../machine/select.md)), so no 128-bit rule is ever taken for a 32-byte vector through the view. Loads and stores of 32 bytes are `vmovdqu`, and a `ymm` copy is `vmovaps`. The frame moves a `ymm` register at 32, 64, 128 and 256 bits by `vmovd`, `vmovq` and `vmovdqu`, unaligned, and a spill slot of 32 bytes realigns the frame before allocation, as any over-aligned local does.

AVX2's unit selects `vpadd`, `vpsub`, `vpand`, `vpor` and `vpxor` of each width, the complement as `vpxor` with the all-ones `vpcmpeqd`, `vpmullw` and `vpmulld`, `vpsllvd`, `vpsllvq`, `vpsrlvd`, `vpsrlvq` and `vpsravd`, and the widening of a lane from an `xmm` register by `vpmovsx` and `vpmovzx`. The rows no one instruction serves are the SSE2 sequences in VEX form: a product of bytes through `vpmullw` and of quadwords through `vpmuludq`, a byte shift one bit of the count at a time, an arithmetic shift of quadwords as `vpsrlvq` of the value flipped by its sign (`vpcmpgtq` against zero), and the conversions between doublewords and binary32. A shift of words takes each half of a doubleword apart: the low word shifted by `vpsllvd`, `vpsrlvd` or `vpsravd` (from the top, then moved down) by the count's low word, and the high word in place by the count's high word, masked and joined, each saturating as the doubleword shifts do past 31. The word shifts and the quadword arithmetic shift are also stated at 16 bytes in VEX.128 form, which under AVX2 costs less than the SSE2 sequences. A comparison has its mask's sign extension as the root, as SSE2's does: `vpcmpeq` and `vpcmpgt` of each width with the operands swapped for the other orders, an unsigned order of 8-, 16- and 32-bit lanes as `vpminu` or `vpmaxu` then `vpcmpeq`, and of 64-bit lanes as both sign bits flipped then `vpcmpgtq`. A splat moves the scalar into an `xmm` register by `vmovd` or `vmovq` and broadcasts it by `vpbroadcast`, an extract takes the lane's half by `vextracti128` then the lane by `vpextr`, and an insert takes the half, writes the lane by `vpinsr` and puts the half back by `vinserti128`. Float lanes are `vaddps`, `vsubps`, `vmulps`, `vdivps` and `vsqrtps` and their `pd` forms, and the compares are `vcmpps` and `vcmppd` with `eq`, `lt`, `le` and `neq`, whose answer on a nan is the scalar's. A float splat is `vbroadcastss` or `vbroadcastsd`, an extract `vextractf128` then `vpshufd`, and an insert `vinsertps`, or `vmovsd` and `vunpcklpd`, then `vinsertf128`. AVX's unit selects the same float arithmetic, a splat by `vpermilps` or `vmovddup` then `vinsertf128`, and the extract and insert. A float comparison is taken lane by lane on such a build, as its mask is integer lanes of 32 bytes no unit of the build holds.

Under System V a vector of 32 bytes that the build holds in a `ymm` register, an `__m256` or `__m256d` under AVX and an `__m256i` under AVX2, is passed whole in the next vector register and returned in `ymm0` (psABI 3.2.3, SSE and three SSEUP). A piece placed in `xmmN` travels in `ymmN` where the value's class is `ymm`, so the copy moves within one class. Windows x64 passes every vector of 32 bytes by reference and returns it through the hidden pointer. Every `ymm` register's upper half is clobbered by a call under both conventions, and Windows' `xmm6` to `xmm15` are kept at 128 bits, which a function writing `ymm6` to `ymm15` saves and restores by their `xmm` views. A `ymm` register has no DWARF number of its own, so a variable held in one is described by its `xmm` view's (17 to 32). `vzeroupper` is not emitted.

A conversion between 32-bit integer and binary32 lanes, and between signed 32-bit integer and binary64 lanes, saturates as the scalar one does. A signed integer converts by `cvtdq2ps` or `cvtdq2pd`, and an unsigned one to binary32 as its high and low 16 bits, each converted exactly, the high half scaled by 2^16 and the two added with one rounding. A float converts to a signed integer by `cvttps2dq` or `cvttpd2dq`, whose indefinite value 0x80000000 is flipped to the greatest value where `cmpps` finds the lane at or above 2^31 and cleared where it is a nan, the binary64 masks narrowed by `pshufd`. A float converts to an unsigned integer by `maxps` against zero, which gives zero for a nan or a negative lane, then `cvttps2dq` of the lane and of the lane less 2^31, the second's top bit restored where the lane is at or above 2^31, and all ones at or above 2^32.

## Cost

A cost is two for each instruction an expansion emits, with a multiply counting three and a divide twelve. It is one less for each instruction the pattern covers, and one less again when the rule takes a constant as an immediate or folds an address into a memory operand.

## What the table does not select

No rule selects a vector operation no row of a unit states (`X86_64_OPERATIONS`, `AVX2_OPERATIONS`, `AVX_OPERATIONS`), as a shuffle, build or reduction of 32 bytes, which the vector legalisation takes lane by lane, nor the signed by unsigned high product, which the multiply legalisation builds, and the call, return, typed address, switch and aggregate operations other legalisations take out.
