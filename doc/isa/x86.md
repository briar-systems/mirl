# x86-64 selection rules

`mirl.target.register.x86.isa.TABLE` is the selection table for x86-64 (see [selection](../machine/select.md)). It is static data. It names rows of masc's x86 catalog and nothing else, so mirl never encodes and never invents an instruction.

## What it reads

- The extensions come from the target's selection, which masc closes over the levels and implied features once. A rule emits rows, and the engine drops a rule whose row masc does not admit. So SSE4.1 (`roundss`, `pmovsx`, `pmovzx`, `pmulld`), LZCNT, BMI1's `tzcnt`, POPCNT and FMA3 are each a row's requirement and never a branch in the table. Where two rules match, the cheaper wins, so the SSE4.1 row is taken where it is admitted and the SSE2 sequence otherwise.
- A rule names each row by the handle masc generates for it (`gen_row_index.ADD_GPR32_GPR32_01_O32`) and each class by its index handle (`gen_register.GPR64_CLASS`). The APX and EVEX forms of a row are never named.

## Values

- Integers, conditions and pointers are held in `gpr64`, floats and vectors in `xmm`. A value is one virtual register of `gpr64`, and a row names it through the view of the width it runs at, `eax`, `ax` or `al` of one location, so one value read as i8, i32 and i64 is never copied between views.
- An integer narrower than 64 bits leaves the bits above it undefined. A condition is 0 or 1 in the whole register. A byte or word operation whose result needs no extension runs at 32 bits, whose write zero extends, so it ends the whole value. A narrower write is a tie, read and written, so no result is ever partly written.
- x86's two-operand forms read and write their first operand, which a rule states as a tie of the result to the operand it replaces. An instruction that merges into its destination, as `cvtsi2sd`, `sqrtss` or `roundss`, follows a whole write of the destination, `movd` or `movaps`.
- The flags are no class. A compare and the branch, `setcc` or `cmov` that reads them are one expansion, and masc's rows state what each reads and writes of `rflags`. A condition held in a register is tested with `test`.
- A comparison clears its result before the compare, then `setcc` writes its low byte. A branch on a compare is `jcc` to the first edge and `jmp` to the second. A float equality is two conditions, zero and no parity, joined.
- Division and the high half of a product use masc's fixed operands: the dividend in `rax`, extended into `rdx` by `cdq`/`cqo` or a zero, the quotient read from `rax` and the remainder from `rdx`. A variable shift moves its count into `ecx` and shifts by `cl`. A byte or a word divides at 32 bits, which the narrow legalisation makes, since the byte and word forms divide `ah:al` and `dx:ax`.
- A float constant is loaded from read-only data, which the pool legalisation makes before selection. A constant an expansion needs, as a sign mask or the bound of a saturating conversion, is built in a general register and moved across.

## Conversions

- Float to integer saturates without a branch: round toward zero, the destination's least or greatest value past its range and 0 for a nan. `cvttss2si` and `cvttsd2si` give the integer indefinite value past the range of their width and for a nan, which `cmov`s on compares against the bound fix up: the greatest value at or above 2^(w-1), and 0 where `ucomis` of the operand with itself is unordered. A narrow signed destination clamps the saturated word. An unsigned destination below 64 bits converts at 64 bits, clamps a negative, a nan or an out of range result to 0, then a value at 2^w or above to the greatest. A 64-bit unsigned destination converts the value less 2^63 where it is at 2^63 or above and sets the top bit.
- Integer to float extends a narrow operand to 32 bits, converts an unsigned 32-bit operand at 64 bits, and converts a 64-bit unsigned operand with its top bit set halved, its low bit kept for the rounding, then doubled.

## Addresses, calls and constants

- A load or a store folds its address into the memory operand: `[b]`, `[b + d]`, `[b + i]`, `[b + i*s]` and `[b + i*s + d]`, the scale a multiply or a shift by a power of two up to 8, and a symbol's address rip-relative with its displacement. `ptr.add` is `lea`.
- The address of a symbol follows the target's relocation model (see [ir/target.md](../ir/target.md)). A symbol reached by its distance from the code, or absolutely, which the small code model keeps within 2 GiB, is rip-relative (`pc32`): an address constant and `func.addr` are `lea`, and a load or a store folds it. A preemptible symbol is a `mov` from its global offset table slot, ELF's `gotpcrel32` and Mach-O's `got_pc32`, and a load or a store goes through the register. Selection states the relocation family and never a refined kind, which masc's encoder refuses: the encoder writes ELF's as its relaxable form for the instruction (`rex_gotpcrel32_relaxable` in a REX `mov`) and Mach-O's as `got_load_pc32` in a 64-bit `mov` load. Only an address at its symbol's start takes either form. A call is `call` with `branch32` under every model, which the format routes through the procedure linkage table.
- A thread-local variable follows the access the model declares. Local exec is a `mov` of the variable's offset from the thread pointer (`tpoff32`) and an `add` of `fs:[0]`, and initial exec a rip-relative `mov` of that offset from the global offset table (`gottpoff32`, which the encoder refines to its `code_4` or `code_6` form under rex2 or evex) and the same `add`. A pc-relative kind is stated only in a rip-relative address and an absolute one only outside one, so `tpoff32` is the `mov`'s immediate. Darwin's variable access is a rip-relative `mov` of the descriptor into rdi (`tlv_pc32`) and a `call` through its thunk (`Callee.descriptor`), which gives the address in rax and leaves only rax, rdi, the flags and the sse registers undefined. General dynamic is not selected, and Windows' TLS index access is refused before selection.
- A direct call is `call rel32` under a branch relocation, and an indirect call `call` through a register. A call's clobbers are its convention row's.

## Atomics

x86-64 is TSO. An atomic load is a plain load at every ordering, and an atomic store a plain store for relaxed and release and `xchg` for seq_cst. An add is `lock xadd`, a sub `lock xadd` of the negation, an exchange `xchg` and a compare and exchange `lock cmpxchg` with the expected value in the accumulator. And, or, exclusive or, nand, minimum and maximum are `cas` loops around `lock cmpxchg` (`mirl.target.register.x86.loop`). Every fence is `mfence`.

## Vectors

An SSE2 vector is held in one `xmm` register. The integer adds and subtracts of each lane width, the bitwise operations, `pmullw`, `pmulld` under SSE4.1 and two `pmuludq` below it, and the float arithmetic of each format are selected. A lane is widened by `pmovsx` and `pmovzx` under SSE4.1, and by `punpckl` with zeros or with the lane's sign below it. Loads and stores move 16, 8 or 4 bytes.

## Cost

A cost is two for each instruction an expansion emits, with a multiply counting three and a divide twelve. It is one less for each instruction the pattern covers, and one less again when the rule takes a constant as an immediate or folds an address into a memory operand.

## What the table does not select

`mirl.target.register.x86.declared` lists the opcodes no rule selects and why: the vector shuffles, builds, inserts, extracts and reductions, which have no rule yet, the signed by unsigned high product, which the multiply legalisation builds, and the call, return, typed address, switch and aggregate operations other legalisations take out.
